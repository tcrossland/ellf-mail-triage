# Mail kinds: annotation guidelines (v0.3)

Pick the **one kind** of each message. The kind is used to decide what to
keep from an old mail archive: work, personal mail and your own records
are kept, automated and bulk mail can go.

## What you see

Each card shows the sender's name and domain, the subject, whether the
message has a `List-Unsubscribe` header, and the start of the body. Judge
from what is shown; don't guess at what the rest of the message says.

## Kinds

Hover a label to see its full definition, what not to mark and examples.

- **WORK**: mail about your job, from people (colleagues, clients,
  suppliers, recruiters) and the job's own paperwork.
- **PERSONAL**: a person you know writing about your life outside work.
- **ADMIN**: your own affairs with organisations, outside work: receipts,
  bookings, statements, contracts, tax, health, school, housing,
  insurance, and a company answering your query.
- **AUTOMATED**: mail a system sends about your own account or activity.
- **BULK**: the same message sent to many people, including mailing
  lists.

## When two kinds seem to fit

Go down this list and take the first that fits:

1. Anything about your job is **WORK**, whoever sent it, even an invoice
   or a booking. Automated mail from a work system is the exception: it
   is **AUTOMATED**.
2. A person you know writing about your life outside work is
   **PERSONAL**.
3. Your own affairs with an organisation are **ADMIN**: a record of
   something you bought, booked, paid or signed, a decision or obligation
   (a tax assessment, an appointment, a claim), or a person there
   answering you.
4. Routine mail a system sends about your account or activity is
   **AUTOMATED**: codes, alerts, shipping updates, reminders.
5. Anything sent the same to many people is **BULK**.

Automated mail from an official body is the hardest case: it is
**ADMIN** when it records a decision or something you must do, and
**AUTOMATED** when it is a routine reminder.

A `List-Unsubscribe` header says the message went out through list or
campaign software. It points to **BULK**, but automated account mail can
carry one too; go by what the message is.

The language of the message doesn't matter, and neither does how old it
is.

## When you're unsure

**Flag the message** (flag icon) whenever you hesitated between two kinds
or none fits well, then pick your best answer and submit. Flagged messages
show where the definitions still need work.
